Research Program · Behavioral Neuroscience · Genetics

Understanding social need and behavioral homeostasis

My research asks how animals integrate social experience with internal physiological states to generate adaptive behavioral decisions. Using Drosophila, I combine behavioral neuroscience, genetics, quantitative behavioral analysis, and neurobiological approaches to uncover the mechanisms that maintain behavioral and physiological balance.

Central concept — social homeostasis.
I propose that animals possess a social homeostatic system that integrates social experience to generate an internal state of social need and coordinates behavioral responses to restore social balance.

The central questions

My research program connects social behavior with broader principles of homeostasis, asking how internal states are generated, sensed, and translated into behavioral actions.

01 · GENERATION

How is social need generated?

How does the nervous system detect changes in social experience and transform them into an internal motivational state?

02 · INTEGRATION

How is social need integrated?

How does social state interact with sleep, feeding, circadian rhythms, and other physiological processes?

03 · ACTION

How is behavioral balance restored?

How do neural circuits and molecular mechanisms coordinate behavioral responses to changing social conditions?

Research directions

These directions form an integrated research program rather than isolated projects. Together, they examine how social experience is translated into changes in behavior and physiology.

01

Social homeostasis and social need

Social interactions provide information that shapes behavioral decisions and physiological states throughout life. My long-term goal is to identify the neural principles by which social experience generates social need and maintains social balance.

  • Identify behavioral signatures of social need and recovery.
  • Determine how recent versus prolonged social experience affects internal state.
  • Identify neural circuits and molecular pathways that sense and regulate social state.
02

Social interactions, sleep, and behavioral homeostasis

Sleep provides a powerful model for testing how social experience interacts with homeostatic regulation. My work examines whether social isolation produces a simple cumulative effect or whether the timing and recency of social loss are more important determinants of sleep.

Recent finding: Recent social loss can transiently reshape sleep, whereas prolonged isolation does not necessarily produce a progressive change in sleep. This suggests that social experience may modulate the expression of sleep homeostasis particularly during acute transitions or elevated sleep pressure.
sociSleep: An identity-preserving tracking and analysis framework that enables quantitative measurement of sleep and social interactions within small groups of Drosophila.
03

Feeding, circadian rhythms, and peripheral clocks

I investigate how feeding behavior and circadian timing interact across neural and peripheral systems. This work examines how feeding schedules influence rhythmic behavior and how specialized gut cells and peripheral clocks contribute to the temporal organization of feeding.

  • Determine how feeding schedules entrain behavioral rhythms.
  • Define contributions of enteroendocrine cells and enterocytes to feeding rhythms.
  • Understand how peripheral clocks respond to prolonged changes in feeding conditions.
04

Quantitative behavioral neuroscience

Many behavioral phenotypes are dynamic, individual, and difficult to capture with conventional assays. I therefore develop computational approaches that make complex behavioral states measurable at high temporal and individual resolution.

  • Identity-preserving tracking of animals in social groups.
  • Automated quantification of sleep, locomotion, proximity, and social interactions.
  • Open and reproducible computational tools for behavioral research.

Experimental and computational approaches

I integrate complementary approaches to connect behavioral phenotypes with underlying mechanisms.

Behavior Social interactions, sleep, feeding, locomotion, and circadian rhythms
Quantification Automated tracking, computational analysis, and behavioral modeling
Genetics Genetic manipulation and cell-type-specific approaches in Drosophila
Neurobiology Neural circuits, neuronal activity, and physiological mechanisms

Future research program

My future laboratory will use Drosophila to investigate social homeostasis as a general biological principle. By combining quantitative behavioral assays with genetics, neural manipulation, and circuit-level analysis, I aim to determine how social experience is encoded in the nervous system and how social need interacts with other homeostatic drives.
